New subscriber

Welcome seriesTurns a new subscriber into a first-time buyer.
  1. Starts when someone joins the newsletter list.
  2. Wait 5 minutes
  3. Email 1WelcomeSays hello and what to expect.Example subject: “Welcome aboard: here's what to expect”
  4. Wait 2 days
  5. Email 2First-order codeA one-time 10% code that expires in 30 days.Example subject: “Your first-order code is ready”
  6. Wait 3 days
  7. Email 3Why this shopThe story behind what the shop sells. No offer.Example subject: “Why we're picky about what we sell”

Rules

  • Everyone added to the list enters once.

Why it is built this way

  • The code waits until day 2. The first email only has to get opened.
  • The last email sells nothing. It gives a reason to stay subscribed.

Watch out

  • Bulk-adding old contacts to the list starts this flow for every one of them.

While shopping

Browse abandonmentBrings back someone who looked and left.
  1. Starts when someone views a product.
  2. Wait 4 hours
  3. Clicked any email in the last 24 hours, or already got this reminder today?Yes: Stops. They are already engaged.No: Continues.
  4. EmailThe second lookShows what they viewed, with a small one-time code.Example subject: “Still thinking it over?”

Rules

  • Skips anyone who started a checkout in the last 4 hours. The cart flow has them.
  • Staff and test addresses are filtered out.

Why it is built this way

  • One email only. Browsing is a weak signal, so it gets a light touch.

Watch out

  • Without the split, a frequent browser gets this every day.
Abandoned cartRecovers a checkout that was started, not finished.
  1. Starts when someone starts a checkout.
  2. Wait 1 hour
  3. Email 1Your cart is savedA plain reminder with a link straight back to the cart.Example subject: “We saved your cart”
  4. Wait 12 hours
  5. Email 2Still holding itAdds a deadline and answers the most common doubt.Example subject: “Your cart won't hold forever”
  6. Wait 12 hours
  7. Email 3Last callFinal reminder, with a 10% code.Example subject: “Last call: 10% off if you finish today”

Rules

  • Anyone who orders leaves the flow at once.
  • The same person can re-enter after 7 days.

Why it is built this way

  • The code only appears in the third email. Shoppers who would buy anyway never see it.
  • All three land inside 25 hours, while the intent is fresh.

Watch out

  • One bad template tag can make every send skip without an error. Check send counts after each template edit.
Price drop alertSays when something they looked at got cheaper.sent at once
  1. Starts when the price of a product drops, by any amount.
  2. EmailIt just got cheaperShows the item, the old price and the new one.Example subject: “Something you looked at just dropped in price”

Rules

  • Goes to people who viewed it or started a checkout with it in the last 60 days.
  • Skips anyone who already bought it.

Why it is built this way

  • It is news, not a promotion. The price change is the offer.

Watch out

  • A one-cent drop is enough to send. Set a minimum if prices move often.
Back in stockTells people a sold-out item has returned.waits for the restock
  1. Starts when someone asks to be told when a sold-out product returns.
  2. Wait until the product is back in stock
  3. EmailIt's backNames the product and links straight to it.Example subject: “Back in stock: the one you asked about”

Rules

  • Sent as a transactional message. They asked for it, so it reaches people who are not on the newsletter.

Why it is built this way

  • The highest-intent email in the set. They asked for this product by name.

Watch out

  • Transactional status needs the platform's approval and locks the email while approved. Plan edits between restocks.
Waitlist alertTells the owner what shoppers are waiting for.sent at once
  1. Starts when someone asks to be told about a sold-out product.
  2. Internal alertNote to the ownerWhich product and which customer, sent to the shop's own inbox.

Rules

  • Internal only. No customer sees it.

Why it is built this way

  • Every signup is a demand signal. It shows what to reorder first.

Watch out

  • A popular product can flood the inbox. Move to a daily summary as volume grows.

After an order

Owner's guide seriesTeaches new customers to use what they bought.
  1. Starts when someone places an order.
  2. Wait 5 days
  3. Email 1Getting startedFirst-use tips, timed to land with the parcel.Example subject: “It should be with you by now: start here”
  4. Wait 3 days
  5. Email 2How to chooseExplains the one spec that matters most.Example subject: “How to read a spec sheet in one minute”
  6. Wait 5 days
  7. Email 3Care and upkeepThe common mistake that shortens the product's life.Example subject: “The mistake that wears it out early”
  8. Wait 10 days
  9. Email 4Make it a habitHow people fit it into an ordinary day.Example subject: “How to actually use it every day”
  10. Wait 10 days
  11. Email 5What to add nextWhat most customers buy second. A soft sell.Example subject: “A month in: what most people add next”

Rules

  • Every order starts the series.

Why it is built this way

  • Four emails teach before one sells.
  • A customer who knows how to use it is less likely to send it back.

Watch out

  • Day 5 is a guess at delivery. Starting from the delivery event is more exact.
Review requestAsks for a review after real use.Built, not switched on
  1. Starts when someone places an order.
  2. Wait 16 days
  3. EmailHow is it working out?One question and one button. About a minute to answer.

Rules

  • There is an empty slot for a thank-you note 30 minutes after the order.

Why it is built this way

  • Sixteen days covers shipping plus real use.

Watch out

  • Kept off while a reviews app sends its own request. Two asks for one review is one too many.
Accessory cross-sellOffers the add-on a new purchase needs.Built, not switched on
  1. Starts when someone places an order.
  2. Wait 3 days
  3. EmailThe missing pieceOffers the matching add-on, with 10% off that category.Example subject: “The one thing your order still needs”

Rules

  • Every order enters. There is no product filter yet.

Why it is built this way

  • Three days in, the order is still top of mind and the parcel is on its way.

Watch out

  • Before switching on, limit it to people who bought the main product without the add-on.
Reorder reminderPrompts a replacement before the old one wears out.Built, not switched on
  1. Starts when someone places an order.
  2. Wait 300 days
  3. EmailAbout ten months inExplains the signs of wear and links the replacement.Example subject: “Yours is about ten months old”

Rules

  • Only orders from the category that wears out.
  • Skipped if they ordered anything in the last 60 days.

Why it is built this way

  • The wait matches how long the product really lasts.
  • It opens with advice, so it reads as service.

Watch out

  • 300 days is one number for a whole category. Split by product when lifespans differ.

Gone quiet

Win-backInvites lapsed customers back before they forget.
  1. Starts when a customer enters the lapsed group: ordered before, nothing in 180 days, still subscribed.
  2. Email 1Come backA 10% code with no minimum.Example subject: “It's been a while: here's 10% to come back”
  3. Wait 7 days
  4. Ordered in the last 7 days?Yes: Stops. They came back.No: Continues.
  5. Email 2Last callSays the code expires in 48 hours.Example subject: “Last call: your 10% ends in 48 hours”

Rules

  • The group refills itself. Nobody is added by hand.

Why it is built this way

  • Nobody who came back gets chased again.
  • Two emails, then silence.

Watch out

  • Set the 180 days from the real gap between orders. Too early annoys regulars. Too late and they are gone.

What to add as the store grows

17 planned flows, in the order they become worth building.

Next

Uses events the store already sends. No new tools.

Add-to-cart reminder

Catches shoppers who add to cart but never reach checkout.

  1. Starts when someone adds a product to the cart.
  2. Wait 3 hours
  3. Started a checkout or ordered since?Yes: Stops. The cart flow or the order has them.No: Continues.
  4. EmailStill in your cartShows the item and links straight back to the cart.Example subject: “You left something in your cart”

What it needs

  • The add-to-cart event from the store platform.

Why

  • It reaches people one step earlier than the abandoned cart flow can.
Delivery-timed guide

Starts the how-to emails when the parcel actually arrives.

  1. Starts when the carrier marks an order delivered.
  2. Wait 1 hour
  3. EmailIt's here: start with thisFirst-use tips, sent the day it lands.
  4. The rest of the owner's guide series follows from this point.

What it needs

  • The delivery event from the store platform or the carrier.

Why

  • Replaces the day-5 guess in the owner's guide series.
Second-order nudge

Earns the second order, the hardest one to get.

  1. Starts when someone places their first order.
  2. Wait 30 days
  3. Ordered again?Yes: Stops.No: Continues.
  4. EmailReady for the next one?Suggests what first-time buyers usually add.

What it needs

  • Nothing new. Order history is already there.

Why

  • A first-time buyer has no habit yet. This gives one reason to return.
Welcome branch for buyers

Stops offering a first-order code to people who already ordered.

  1. Starts when someone joins the newsletter list (a split at the top of the welcome series).
  2. Has ordered before?Yes: Gets a thank-you and the owner's guide instead of the code.No: Gets the normal welcome series.

What it needs

  • Nothing new. One split added to the welcome series.

Why

  • Existing customers join the list too. A first-order code tells them the shop does not know them.
Sunset

Stops emailing people who no longer open.

  1. Starts when someone enters the unengaged group: no opens or clicks in 120 days.
  2. EmailStill want these?One clear button to stay subscribed.Example subject: “Still want to hear from us?”
  3. Wait 7 days
  4. Clicked to stay?Yes: Stops. They stay on the list.No: Continues.
  5. Marketing emails to them are switched off.

What it needs

  • A group built from opens and clicks.

Why

  • Inbox providers judge a sender by how many people engage.
  • Klaviyo bills by active profiles, so a clean list also costs less.
Low-stock alert

Warns interested shoppers when an item is nearly gone.

  1. Starts when a product's stock falls below a set number (a built-in trigger).
  2. EmailAlmost goneShows the item and how few are left. No discount.Example subject: “Almost gone: the one you looked at”

What it needs

  • Stock levels synced from the store platform.

Why

  • Honest scarcity. It only sends when stock is truly low.
Refund follow-up

Turns a refund into feedback.

  1. Starts when an order is refunded.
  2. Wait 1 day
  3. EmailWhat went wrong?One question. Replies go to a person.Example subject: “Sorry it didn't work out. What went wrong?”

What it needs

  • The refund event from the store platform.

Why

  • The answers show what to fix on the product page.

Then

Worth it once there are enough repeat buyers to group.

VIP thank-you

Rewards the best customers before they ask.

  1. Starts when someone enters the VIP group, for example three or more orders.
  2. EmailYou're one of our regularsThanks them. Gives early access to new arrivals, not a discount.

What it needs

  • Enough repeat buyers for the group to mean something.

Why

  • Regulars buy without discounts. Recognition keeps it that way.
Purchase anniversary

Marks a year since the first order.

  1. Starts when the date of someone's first order comes around (a date-based trigger).
  2. EmailOne year inShows what is new since they bought. Suggests an upgrade or a replacement.Example subject: “One year in: here's what's new”

What it needs

  • The first-order date saved on each profile.

Why

  • A reason to write that is about them, not about a sale.
Cross-sell by category

Recommends the right next product for what was just bought.

  1. Starts when someone places an order.
  2. Wait 7 days
  3. Splits by the category they bought. Each path has its own recommendation.
  4. EmailGoes well with yoursThe match for their category, or best sellers if there is none.Example subject: “Goes well with what you bought”

What it needs

  • Tidy product categories.
  • One recommendation set per category.

Why

  • Replaces the single accessory cross-sell with one that fits every order.
Smarter cart branches

Treats big carts and returning customers differently.

  1. Starts when someone starts a checkout (splits inside the abandoned cart flow).
  2. Is the cart large?Yes: Gets a personal note and an offer of help.No: Gets the standard reminders.
  3. Has ordered before?Yes: Gets reminders with no code.No: Gets the code in the last email.

What it needs

  • Enough carts each week to see a difference between paths.

Why

  • Discounts go only where they change the outcome.
Review to referral

Asks happy reviewers to bring a friend.

  1. Starts when someone leaves a five-star review.
  2. Wait 1 day
  3. EmailKnow someone who'd like it?Thanks them and gives a link to share.Example subject: “Thanks for the review. Know someone who'd like it?”

What it needs

  • A reviews app that sends review events to Klaviyo.

Why

  • The ask lands at the moment they are happiest with the shop.

Later

Needs a new channel or Klaviyo's predictions.

Text-message steps

Adds a text where minutes matter.

  1. Starts when inside existing flows: abandoned cart, back in stock, delivery.
  2. Text messageShort textGoes to people who agreed to texts. Email stays as the fallback.

What it needs

  • A sending number.
  • Text-message consent collected at signup or checkout.

Why

  • A restock can sell out before an email gets opened.
Predicted reorder

Reminds each customer near the date they are likely to reorder.

  1. Starts when a few days before Klaviyo's predicted next-order date for that person.
  2. EmailAbout time for another?Shows their last order with a link to reorder it.

What it needs

  • Klaviyo's predictive analytics. At the time of writing that takes about 500 customers with orders, 180 days of order history, orders in the last 30 days, and some customers with three or more orders.

Why

  • Replaces the fixed 300-day wait with a date per person.
Churn-risk save

Reaches customers who are drifting, before they lapse.

  1. Starts when someone enters the high churn-risk group.
  2. Email 1Anything we can help with?Useful content first. No offer.
  3. Wait 7 days
  4. Ordered since?Yes: Stops.No: Continues.
  5. Email 2A small thank-youA modest offer for staying.Example subject: “A small thank-you for sticking around”

What it needs

  • Klaviyo's predictive analytics, as for the predicted reorder.

Why

  • Win-back waits 180 days. This acts when the risk first shows.
Alerts to team chat

Sends owner alerts to chat or a helpdesk instead of an inbox.

  1. Starts when any internal alert: waitlist signups, refunds, large orders.
  2. WebhookPost to team chatSends the product, the customer and a link.

What it needs

  • A chat tool or helpdesk that accepts webhooks.

Why

  • Alerts get seen where the team already works.
Standing A/B tests

Keeps one test running on the busiest flows.

  1. Starts when inside the welcome series and the abandoned cart flow.
  2. Tests one thing at a time: send time, subject line, or code against no code.
  3. Keeps the winner, then starts the next test.

What it needs

  • Enough sends each week to reach a clear result.

Why

  • Small gains on the busiest flows add up faster than a new flow.
